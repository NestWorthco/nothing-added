How the scale works
  1. Single ingredient. One thing, nothing else. 100% cocoa, plain rolled oats, frozen peas.
  2. Whole foods only. Several whole ingredients, none refined or added. Muesli of oats, nuts and dried fruit.
  3. Kitchen-made. Whole foods plus staples you'd add at home: salt, sugar, oil, butter, vinegar, honey.
  4. Processed. Contains refined or extracted ingredients (white flour, syrups, milk solids, isolates) or functional additives with a code number.
  5. Ultra-processed. Contains cosmetic additives: emulsifiers, thickeners, flavours, colours, sweeteners, flavour enhancers, modified starch, or a long list of additives.

The purity score (0–100) ranks products within each step: fewer and gentler additions score higher. Additive numbers on NZ and AU labels are International Numbering System (INS) codes, the same as E-numbers without the E. Ingredients are listed by weight, heaviest first. NIP levels use per-100g food thresholds: sugars over 22.5 g is high and 5 g or under is low; sodium over 600 mg is high and 120 mg or under is low.

Daily targets

Defaults are the Daily Intake values behind the %DI on NZ and AU labels (Food Standards Code 1.2.8), based on an average adult eating 8,700 kJ a day. Protein and fibre are goals to reach. Fat, saturated fat, sugars and sodium are limits to stay under. Change them to suit you.
